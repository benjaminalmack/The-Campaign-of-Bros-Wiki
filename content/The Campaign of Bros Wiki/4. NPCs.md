#  Winkus Thricebound of Neverwinter

A gnome bard with untamed ginger curls, a lute shaped like a pineapple, and the attention span of a raccoon on sugar. He's on a "legend hunt" for stories too absurd to be true—until he sees one of your adventurers with a third leg. His muse strikes instantly.

Armor Class 13 (leather armor)
Hit Points 22 (5d6 + 5)
Speed 25 ft.
STR 8 (-1)
DEX 14 (+2)
CON 12 (+1)
INT 10 (+0)
WIS 11 (+0)
CHA 17 (+3)
Saving Throws Dex +4, Cha +5
Skills Performance +7, Insight +2, Persuasion +5
Senses Darkvision 60 ft., Passive Perception 10
Languages Common, Gnomish, Elvish
Challenge 1/4 (50 XP)
Proficiency Bonus +2

# Varnoth
Before Dragon’s Rest, before Stormwreck Isle, she served with the Azure Wolves, a disciplined mercenary company operating north of the Sword Coast during the mid–15th century DR. The Wolves were not sellswords in the crude sense; they specialized in containment contracts, threats that kingdoms did not want named, acknowledged, or remembered, like Fiends, Aberrations, and, in general, things that should not linger.  She was a tracker and interdiction specialist, the person you sent after something escaped.
## Varnoth's Past with the Barghest
The Azure Wolves ended up being  hired by a coalition of trade cities to investigate disappearances along a mountain pass. Caravans of noble families vanished. Survivors spoke of a wolf that walked like a man and spoke like a judge.  

At first, we assumed it was a werewolf, but they were wrong. Within three nights of arrival, two veteran Wolves were found drained and hollowed, their bodies intact but empty.
Campfires went out on their own.  Something began calling soldiers by name, names it should not have known.  That was when the company’s arcanist identified the truth: A [[10. Monsters and Fiends#Barghest|Barghest]].

When finally confronted, the Barghest was able to escape through a planar tear, an unstable rupture in the fabric of reality that creates a direct, often uncontrolled, gateway between the Material Plane and another plane of existence.  

Living on Dragon's Rest, the creature appears to have made its way into Varnoth's life once more.  She saw a lone traveler, what appeared to be only a goblin at first, arrive at Dragon’s Rest asking about a man named Levi Jaegor.  However, Varnoth noticed the signs: no fear of dragons, no interest in coin, and questions framed like judgments. The true tell was when Varnoth inhaled the creature’s lingering scent: cold, sulfurous, wrong. Varnoth knew immediately, this was not a monster, but a fiend.  This was a Barghest.
